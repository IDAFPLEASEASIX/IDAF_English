    PCs aren't as scary as they look

    Short, plain-English answers to the things people worry about most.

    What does each part actually do?
    • CPU: the brain. It runs programs and tells everything else what to do.
    • GPU (graphics card): draws the picture. It matters most for games, video editing and 3D work.
    • RAM: short-term memory. More lets you keep more things open at once.
    • Storage (SSD): long-term memory. Your files, programs and Windows live here.
    • Motherboard: the circuit board that everything plugs into.
    • PSU (power supply): turns wall power into the power your parts use.
    • Case: the box. It also decides how well air can flow through.
    Will these parts fit together?

    Three checks catch most mistakes:

    • Socket: the CPU and motherboard must use the same socket (for example AM5 or LGA1700). Our picker checks this for you.
    • RAM type: DDR4 and DDR5 are not interchangeable. The motherboard supports only one.
    • Physical size: check the graphics card length and the cooler height against the case's listed limits.
    How big a power supply do I need?

    Add up the CPU and graphics card power, add about 100 W for everything else, then leave 25% spare. That spare room keeps the PSU quiet and efficient and leaves space for upgrades.

    Don't buy the cheapest unit to save money. The PSU feeds every other part, so a bad one can damage them.

    DDR4 or DDR5?

    DDR5 is the newer standard and what new platforms are built around. DDR4 boards and memory cost less, which helps on a tight budget, but you can't move that RAM to a DDR5 board later. If you plan to upgrade over several years, pick DDR5.

    Where should I spend, and where can I save?

    Spend on: the graphics card if you game, enough RAM (16 GB minimum, 32 GB if you edit or multitask a lot), and a decent PSU.

    Save on: flashy cases, RGB lighting, and the most expensive CPU. A mid-range CPU paired with a stronger graphics card usually gives better gaming results than the reverse.

    Can I really build it myself?

    Yes. Every connector is shaped so it only fits one way, so if you have to force something, stop and check. Most people finish in two to three hours. The order that makes it easiest:

    • Install the CPU, cooler and RAM on the motherboard while it sits on its box, outside the case.
    • Fit the PSU, then screw the motherboard into the case.
    • Add the SSD and graphics card.
    • Connect the power cables and the small front-panel connectors last. Check the motherboard manual for where each goes.
    Thermal paste, coolers and static

    Use a pea-sized dot of thermal paste in the middle of the CPU, and don't spread it. Many coolers come with paste already applied, so check first. Static damage is rare: touch the metal case before handling parts and you're fine. Hold cards and boards by the edges.

    Don't forget the small things
    • Plug the monitor into the graphics card, not the motherboard.
    • Remove the plastic film from the cooler's base.
    • Push RAM in until both clips click.
    • Plug in both CPU power and the main motherboard power cable.
    • You need a USB stick of at least 8 GB to install Windows.
    It won't turn on or there's no picture

    Work through these in order, because the cause is usually simple:

    • Check the PSU's back switch is on and the cable is firmly in the wall.
    • Re-seat the RAM and make sure it clicked in.
    • Confirm the monitor cable is in the graphics card.
    • Check that the CPU power cable (4 or 8 pin, near the top of the board) is plugged in.

    If you hear beeps, look up the pattern in your motherboard manual. Each one names a specific problem.

    My PC is loud or running hot

    Make sure the front fans pull air in and the back fan pushes it out. Keep cables away from the airflow path and clean dust filters every few months. In the BIOS you can set a gentler fan curve, which is often all it takes to quiet a PC down.

    Is buying used parts safe?

    Used graphics cards and CPUs can be good value. Be careful with PSUs, which wear out and can fail dangerously, and with graphics cards that were used for mining or have no receipt. Always test a used card right away so you're still inside the seller's return window.
